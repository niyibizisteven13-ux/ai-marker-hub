# Marking a quiz, CAT or exam

**Will it be marked?** In the form builder, "Will this be marked (a quiz, CAT or exam)?" turns marking on. Then **Scoring and result** asks:
- **What is it?** Quiz, CAT, Test or Exam, and a **name** for the result ("CAT 1").
- **Report out of** (optional): a CAT is often out of 10, 20 or 30. The mark is the percentage scaled to that, to the nearest half mark. Empty = the total of the question points.
- **Pass mark (%)**, default 50.
- **Mark written answers with AI**.

**What is marked how**
- Choice, true/false and short answers are marked **at once** against the answer key.
- **Written (long) answers** are marked by **you**, or, with the AI switch on, by **AI** against the *Model answer or marking points* you write under the question. The AI gives marks (half marks, never more than the question is worth) and one or two sentences of feedback, and says when it is not sure. Every AI mark is labelled **AI mark** in Results and you can change it (Marks → Save mark): your mark always wins and is never replaced by AI. An empty answer is 0 without asking the AI. Students are told their score may change while written answers wait. Responses that came in before you switched AI marking on can be marked with **Mark waiting written answers with AI** (40 at a time).
- A student's score is kept with the response: **score, percentage, grade, mark out of, pass or fail**. Results, the Excel file and the class gradebook all use it.

**Grades** (default scale, shown on results): A 80+, B 70+, C 60+, D 50+, E 40+, F below 40.

**Honest limits.** AI marking of written answers is a first marker, not a substitute for you: it can be wrong, especially on short or unusual answers, so check the ones marked "not sure" and a sample of the rest. Long answers with no model answer are marked on correctness and completeness only. The gradebook counts a written answer as 0 until it is marked.
